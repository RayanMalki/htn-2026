One of the main tips we're here for improving our health is to stay hydrated. But does that mean to just increase our water intake? Not necessarily. Here I want to address two of the main myths that we hear about water and hydration. The first one is that 60% of the body is composed of water. Well not quite. We don't just have pure water in our bodies. It's combined with electrolytes to create flus like blood and urine. Electrolytes are minerals such as sodium, calcium, potassium among others. So if we want to be properly hydrated, we need to make sure we're getting enough minerals from our diet. The second myth is that we're supposed to drink eight glass of water a day. I couldn't find any scientific evidence to support this claim, which is why I just drink when I'm thirsty. The body has the incredible capacity to maintain its natural balance. If you're not having enough, you'll be thirsty. If you're having too much, your body will get rid of it through urine. In the end, hydration is about having enough water and minerals to have them work together and listen to the body to know how much water it needs.
