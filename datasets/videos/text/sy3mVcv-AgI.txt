While the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention hailed fluoride as one of the top great public health achievements, a new study raises questions about its role in children's development. The U.S. Public Health Service considers 0.7 milligrams of fluoride per liter of drinking water an optimal level. But the study in the journal JAMA Pediatrics suggests increased levels of fluoride exposure during pregnancy are associated with declines of IQ in children. Previous research shows similar findings. The study assesses more than 500 child and mother pairs. It finds for every one milligram per liter average increase in fluoride intake by mother. There was a 3.7 drop in the child's IQ regardless of gender. Katie Johnston WPC News.
