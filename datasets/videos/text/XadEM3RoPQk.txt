It can be a symptom of obstructive sleep apnea. That's when you have pauses in breathing while you sleep. This can lead to elevated cortisol levels. Cortisol is the body's primary stress hormone, and its levels naturally decrease during restorative sleep. Poor sleep interrupts this cycle, leading to higher cortisol production. The combination of snoring, sleep disruption, and elevated cortisol levels constrain the cardiovascular system, increasing the risk of heart disease, stroke, and other related conditions. And if that was enough, it can also cause reduced insulin sensitivity. Making people with sleep apnea or severe snoring at a higher risk for diabetes. Many of these conditions go unnoticed, but you may recognize symptoms like fatigue, irritability, and reduced cognitive function during the day.
