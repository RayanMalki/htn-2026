Graves' disease is an autoimmune disorder. An autoimmune disorder is when your immune system starts to attack parts of your body because it thinks they don't belong. In Graves' disease, the immune system makes a very specific antibody that sticks onto the thyroid. Anibody is special because it sticks onto an on-switch for the thyroid cell, telling it to make a lot of thyroid hormone. This is why people with Graves' disease are hyperthyroid, meaning they have too much thyroid hormone. Thyroid hormone affects several parts of the body. It goes to places like the heart, skeletal muscle, eyes, skin, bones, and liver. So having too much thyroid hormone causes many whole body effects. These symptoms can include anxiety and irritability, a fine tremor or shaking of the hands, or fingers, sensitivity to heat, and an increase in sweating or warm moist skin, weight loss, despite normal eating habits, enlargement of the thyroid gland or goiter, a change in menstrual cycles, erectile dysfunction or reduced sexual drive, and frequent bowel movements.
